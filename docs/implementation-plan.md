# Implementation plan

## Shared foods and reliable logging (2026-10-22)

**Implemented design.** Each unique reusable food belongs in a general database and personal libraries link to it. Keep personal meals, quantities, names, IDs, transcripts and recovery payloads out of repository files. This document records technical contracts; it does not authorize deployment or personal-data changes.

**Required model:** one general food identity with versioned nutritional/source/preparation facts, and a separate profile-to-food link for personal use. The same confirmed product or recipe can be linked by several profiles without cloning its definition. Diary portions, dates, personal names/notes/preferences, body records and goals remain profile-owned. Shared storage does not automatically make private recipes or personal metadata publicly discoverable. Retain imported source records unchanged and connect them to food identities/evidence; do not insert user estimates as if a source publisher supplied them.

**Identity and correction decisions:** exact eligible barcode/catalogue identity plus relevant variant/preparation can resolve a common food; similar or translated names alone cannot prove identity. Unkeyed basics and recipes need resolved identity or an explicit confirmation, not a claimed universal semantic deduplication guarantee. Define conflict/version rules before implementation: a changed label or differing source observation must not silently rewrite another profile's chosen values or historical diary snapshots. Reuse the current authenticated API, atomic retention and versioned/idempotent corrections. Use confirmed servings rather than inventing cooked weight. Preserve unknown/partial nutrients and actual provenance. A mass portion against a volume-based label needs a supported conversion or an explicit estimate; a food link does not make an estimated conversion label evidence. No new search service or nutrition engine.

**Reviewed architecture:** retain the existing product/recipe IDs as personal library links, moving reusable facts into immutable revisions of one shared food identity. Personal names, recognition text, notes, ingredient names/notes and catalogue preferences stay on those links. Recipe ingredient facts belong to the shared recipe revision and reference shared product revisions, so deleting another person's link cannot break them. Entries retain independent snapshots and pin the revision used; migration does not recalculate them. Exact eligible keys converge globally; unkeyed/legacy identities stay separate. Conflicting facts become separate revisions, and choosing a different revision for an existing personal link remains an explicit versioned correction.

**Discovery and deletion:** the imported catalogue remains the sole publisher-record store. Revisions reference its existing IDs; imports are never rewritten or copied into another catalogue. User definitions have no public browsing route. A connection may discover/reuse definitions through profiles already approved on that connection, with current membership checked again when linking. Website reads stay limited to the signed-in profile. Shared lookup returns no personal notes. New links never inherit another profile's notes/preferences. Deletion removes personal links/metadata; shared facts remain while any diary, library or retained recipe needs their identity. Unreferenced identities are removed transactionally, without deleting imported evidence.

**Concurrency and workflow:** keep request replay and profile locking; serialize the small shared-food mutation section with a transaction advisory lock and enforce database uniqueness for identities, revisions and profile links. Use one existing food reference, one atomic new/link reference, or one explicit diary-only exception per new entry. There is no separate retention boolean or confirmation badge. Require product references for newly retained recipe ingredients, retain the original nullable legacy references and return per-entry resolution evidence. A translated-name miss returns bounded personal fallback candidates (including unclassified foods), clearly marked as candidates rather than matches; chat then checks brand/barcode and permitted shared definitions before catalogue lookup.

**Implementation sequence:**

1. Design the general food identities/revisions and profile-link persistence against the existing catalogue, products, recipes, references, deletion and source contracts. Extend the existing model rather than adding an independent competing catalogue. Put uniqueness constraints and concurrent resolution on the common identity, and uniqueness on each profile-food association. Migrate existing reusable records and references without recomputing snapshots, promoting personal notes into shared data or merging ambiguous identities. Deterministic exact duplicates may converge on a common identity; conflicting evidence retains its own revision and ambiguous matches require review. Confirm the migration map and privacy/deletion behavior before execution.
2. Tighten lookup in API/tools and the existing skill: explicit profile, personal links first, then permitted general-food lookup by identity/brand/barcode; fall back when a localized name misses. A general match adds a personal link rather than another food definition. Include legacy unclassified candidates without assigning guessed metadata. Read chosen definitions and use confirmed recognition names; do not treat an empty translated query or product-only filter as absence.
3. Close the structural retention gap in `diary/schemas.ts`, `retainedFoods.ts`, `meals.controller.ts` and `mcp/tools.ts` against the shared model. Require an existing authorized food/link reference, a newly resolved food reference, or an explicit `diary_only_reason` (`one_off_restaurant` or `insufficient_recipe_details`) for each new/appended entry. Reject contradictory or absent decisions before committing. Retained recipes resolve their ingredients to common food definitions while their use is authorized through the selected profile. Resolve/create shared definitions, profile links and diary entries atomically. Reason validation makes the decision explicit; it cannot prove that the model classified the meal truthfully.
4. Return compact per-entry food-resolution and profile-link outcomes as well as the meal ID. Return recipe retention only when a recipe exists. Update the existing skill to verify these results, preserve per-person breakfast labels, keep source/basis facts consistent and report precise failures without claiming an unsaved meal was saved. Investigate the original connector error from actual error details before selecting any additional patch; recovery does not depend on retrying that chat.
5. Update the product spec and existing documentation for the approved contract. Update the same private plugin and refresh its tools after the matching API is deployed; preserve its identity, binding and audience. Existing chats using older schemas may require the refreshed catalogue before their next write; do not leave a legacy bypass or silently relax validation.

**File map:** `apps/api/src/db/schema.ts`, reviewed generated migrations, `apps/api/src/diary/{schemas,records,foods,views,retainedFoods,meals.controller}.ts`, affected product/recipe and account-deletion handlers, `apps/api/src/mcp/tools.ts`, existing plugin skill/release metadata, existing identity/migration/food/MCP/diary/retention tests and website API/read-model tests. ### Implementation contracts

#### Reusable foods and identity (U3–U5)

Keep the existing product/recipe tables and IDs. Basics and packaged products share the product table with `food_kind: basic | product`; recipes retain their ingredient snapshots and whole-recipe values. Add optional `variant` (200 characters), `recognition_description` (1,000 characters) and `preparation: { state: as_sold | as_published | other | unspecified, description: string | null }` to products; preparation descriptions have at most 200 characters and `other` requires one. Recipes gain a recognition description; their evidence remains on the ingredients, without inventing a single label for a calculated recipe. New automatically retained recipes need ingredients plus a usable yield amount/unit or servings; an underspecified dish stays diary-only until chat obtains that information.

Product `provenance` holds `source: label | dataset | estimate`, with the existing label-basis/values shape for `label` and one exact `catalogue_record_id` for `dataset`. New definitions require kind, preparation state and provenance; updates may preserve null metadata on an older row rather than inventing evidence. The product's own basis/nutrients remain the supplied reusable values; the existing note explains assumptions or mixed origins. Preferred record IDs remain lookup preferences, not proof that all their values were used. Reusing a food copies the relevant evidence into the new entry snapshot in chat; changing a template never rewrites an entry.

| Identity supplied by chat | Stored key, scoped by profile | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing product/recipe ID | Existing ID, with current ownership checked | Reuse it; never create another definition for a changed eaten amount. |
| Packaged product in its `as_sold` state with an eligible barcode | `gtin:<14-digit normalized GTIN>` using the existing `gtinKey` validator | The code identifies the sold product. Different preparation uses a separate unkeyed food or its own exact catalogue record. Barcode validity is independent of nutrition certainty. |
| One chosen catalogue record in its `as_published` state | `catalogue:<record ID>`; the record already identifies its release | For an as-sold packaged product found through a catalogue, supply `as_sold` and its barcode so the preceding GTIN rule applies. Otherwise keep the exact record key; do not silently merge source releases. |
| No reliable key, including a recipe or food changed from the source preparation | No identity key | Search the person's saved foods first and reuse the intended ID. Ask when several candidates fit. A new definition is allowed when none fits; only same-request replay is guaranteed here, not semantic deduplication across different requests. |

The server derives the key from validated evidence, never from a caller-chosen opaque key, name, note, nutrient hash, image or fuzzy preparation match. Do not create a GTIN key for restricted/internal/invalid codes. Store at most one key per product; expose it in reads for diagnostics and reuse. Enforce unique `(account_id, identity_key)` for non-null keys. Recipes have no computed ingredient/name key in this batch.

On a key hit, compare the supplied `food_kind`, brand/variant, preparation, reusable basis/unit, nutrient values (including absent versus zero) and provenance with the current definition, using exact validated values. Identical content reuses the ID/version and does not overwrite its name, recognition description or note. Conflicting content returns `409 food_identity_conflict` with the current owned product/version and client reference; the entire meal command rolls back. Chat then reads/reuses that product, explicitly updates it with its version, or corrects the identity/preparation; it must not bypass the collision by silently dropping the key. The service does not convert units or decide nutritional equivalence to make definitions match.

Standalone product create/update routes use the same identity rules as meal retention. An explicit versioned update may correct a product and its key if the new key is free; it conflicts with another product's key in this profile and never merges rows or alters old meals. Existing products migrate with null new classification/provenance/preparation/key fields: show “not recorded” where relevant. Do not backfill identity, infer kind from names or deduplicate current rows. A later explicit chat correction can fill missing facts; collision recovery uses the existing keyed ID, leaving unrelated historic rows intact.

#### One meal command, including append (U4)

Extend both `POST /profiles/:id/meals` and `POST /profiles/:id/meals/:mealId/entries`, and both branches of `save_meal`, with optional `new_foods`. Each definition has a unique `client_ref` (1–64 ASCII letters/digits/underscore/hyphen), `type: product | recipe`, and its validated definition. Entries refer to either an existing product/recipe ID or one `food_ref` client reference, never multiple reusable references. Recipe ingredients may refer to existing products or new product references; nested recipe references/cycles are not supported. Keep the current limits of 100 added entries and 200 ingredients per recipe; allow at most 100 new definitions per command. Reject duplicate/unresolved references, wrong-profile IDs and definitions unused by the saved entries/ingredients before committing.

Resolve product definitions before recipe definitions, then persist the meal/entries and all resolved references in the same `WriteService` transaction. The response preserves the existing `meal` and append `created_entry_ids`, and adds `retained_foods: [{ client_ref, type, id, version, outcome: created | reused }]`. Chat reports retention only from that result. Entry quantities/nutrients/source snapshots are supplied by chat; the server neither scales entries from a template nor copies over conflicting supplied values. A deliberate repeat has a new `request_id` and creates a new meal; retries reuse the original ID/payload and return the same meal and food mappings.

Add a profile-scoped transaction advisory lock in `WriteService` for every profile data mutation, after the existing request-ID replay check and before the write callback. This serializes writes for one diary across different request IDs/connections; other profiles and reads remain independent. Keep lock order request ID → profile → meal → entries, including reference edits and product/recipe deletes. The food unique constraint is the persistent invariant; request-ID locking alone is insufficient. Product/recipe replacement stays a separate versioned write, not a hidden side effect of additive logging.

Add a nullable cascading profile foreign key to `request_results` and set it for every new profile-scoped cached write; profile-less invite results remain null. This makes deletion remove cached copies of the newly stored foods/body/goal data even when other profiles keep the connection alive. Preserve existing request-ID results during migration; do not clear the replay cache or claim that old unscoped results were backfilled. Test new-response cleanup and failed replay after profile removal explicitly.

#### Body context, profile name and agreed goals (U6–U7)

`GET /api/v1/profiles/:profileId` / `read_profile` returns the profile's name/version and `body_context`, null before first save. `PATCH /api/v1/profiles/:profileId/body-context?version=N` / `update_body_context` accepts only the optional fields below. A new singleton body-context row uses `version=0` as the create precondition and starts at 1; existing rows require their positive version. Enforce one row per profile and a cascading profile foreign key. Use `WriteService`, the profile lock and the existing authorization boundary; concurrent initial creates or stale edits return `409 version_conflict` with the current context, not an upsert overwrite.

| Body field | Stored shape | Patch/clear behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `height` | `{ value: positive number, unit: cm | in }` or null | Replace/clear the whole pair; no server conversion. |
| `date_of_birth` | ISO calendar date or null, for an adult aged 18 or over | Optional supplied fact; preserve on omission and clear only with explicit null. Older target snapshots can omit it. |
| `age` | `{ years: integer >= 18, as_of: ISO date }` or null | Replace/clear the pair; require the observation date and do not derive a current age. |
| `activity` | Non-empty text up to 1,000 characters or null | Save the supplied description, not an inferred activity multiplier. |
| `goal` | Non-empty text up to 1,000 characters or null | Save the person's stated goal; this does not activate nutrient targets. |

Every patch has at least one field; omitted fields remain unchanged and explicit null clears only that field. Clearing everything keeps the row/version with empty fields. Creation, updates and returned views share these schemas; the approved optional birth date is included, but no sex or catch-all health fields are added. Body shows age with “as of” available in details and never suggests that old context has been freshly confirmed.

**Access and security**

- **AC-1** Superseded by OA-5 in the next version. `/api/v1` accepts only connection credentials; `/api/manage` accepts only Firebase ID tokens; either credential is refused at the other door.
- **AC-2** Diary routes are `/api/v1/accounts/{accountId}/…`. Every request resolves credential hash → non-revoked connection → current grant, including before replaying a cached write result. Personal record IDs and product/recipe references must belong to that account. Unknown and ungranted accounts both return 403 `account_not_available`.
- **AC-3** Credential: `fdk_` + 32 random bytes (base64url), shown once, stored as SHA-256 with `last_used_at`. Replace swaps the hash in place; revoke is permanent.
- **AC-4** Shareable connection ID: `conn_` + 12 random base32 characters, separate from the internal ID.
- **AC-5** Every manage request calls `verifyIdToken(token, true)`, which rejects disabled users and revoked sessions. Ordinary sign-out only ends the browser session; an already-issued ID token stays valid until it expires (one hour). To cut a person off immediately, an operator revokes their refresh tokens with the Admin SDK or disables the user in Firebase, and their next manage request fails; v1 has no website button for this. Connection credentials are independent: revoking a person's Firebase session leaves connections working, and revoking a connection leaves sign-in working. The account is created at first verified sign-in, keyed by Firebase UID.
- **AC-6** Sign-in uses `signInWithPopup`. If popups are blocked or fail in iPhone Safari, switch to `signInWithRedirect` and handle `getRedirectResult` on return, using Firebase redirect option 3: set `authDomain` to our hostname, have Caddy transparently proxy `/__/auth/` to `<project>.firebaseapp.com`, and add `https://<hostname>/__/auth/handler` as an authorized redirect URI for the Google OAuth client. Option 1 needs Firebase Hosting, which we do not use.
- **AC-7** Firewall allows ports 22, 80 and 443 only; SSH by key; PostgreSQL is reachable only inside Compose; secrets live in a root-only `.env` and the service-account file, never in git; automatic security updates are on.

**Diary data**

- **DA-1** UUIDv7 keys for personal rows; bigint keys for catalogue rows.
- **DA-2** Diary and weight dates are plain dates supplied by chat as the person's local day; server timestamps are UTC; the server does no time-zone logic.
- **DA-3** A `nutrients` object with the spec's eight fixed keys and units (`energy_kcal`, `protein_g`, `carbohydrate_g`, `sugars_g`, `fat_g`, `saturated_fat_g`, `fibre_g`, `salt_g`). A missing key means unknown.
- **DA-4** Entries store values for the eaten amount as supplied, plus quantity and free-text unit; the server never recalculates.
- **DA-5** `source` is `label`, `dataset` or `estimate`, plus a note. `dataset` requires a catalogue record; `label` requires the label basis and values copied onto the entry. Product and recipe references are provenance only.
- **DA-6** Editable rows carry `version`; updates and deletes send the version read; a mismatch returns 409 `version_conflict` with the current row.
- **DA-7** Deletes are hard; deleting a meal deletes its entries. Deleting a product or recipe preserves logged entries and their value/source snapshots. Grant removal and connection revocation never delete diary data.
- **DA-8** A day's target is the newest variant of the selected kind whose `effective_from` is on or before that day. A day with no selection uses the normal-day variant.
- **DA-9** Every chat-data write carries a chat-generated `request_id`, reused on retry. Its canonical fingerprint covers the method, path (including account and target IDs), validated query parameters such as `version`, and body. Store the result per connection in the same transaction as the write; after current authorization checks, replay it only for an identical fingerprint. A reused ID with a different fingerprint returns 409 `request_id_reused`. Credential-issuing management responses are outside this cache; plaintext credentials are never persisted.
- **DA-10** Recipes: name, yield amount and unit, servings, whole-recipe nutrients as supplied, note and `version`. Ingredients: position, name, quantity, unit, source, note, optional catalogue or product reference, and nutrients. Saving a recipe replaces its ingredient list in one write.
- **DA-11** Usual products: name, brand, raw barcode (plus a CA-2 grouping key when eligible), basis amount and unit, label nutrients per basis, preferred catalogue records, note and `version`. Weight entries: date, value, unit (`kg` or `lb`) as supplied, and note.

**Catalogue**

- **CA-1** `catalogue_release` (source, release, archive SHA-256, read/imported/rejected counts), `catalogue_record` (unique on release + source ID; raw barcode, normalised GTIN, name, brand, serving basis and preparation as supplied, nutrients) and `nutrient_definition` per release.
- **CA-2** GTIN normalisation per the spec: ASCII digits only, length 8, 12, 13 or 14 and a valid GS1 check digit. Exclude restricted-circulation numbers using their underlying format, including zero-padded representations: RCN-8 prefixes 0/2; RCN-13 prefixes 02/04/20–29 and corresponding RCN-12 forms; the internal UPC company prefixes 000000 and 001000–007999. Only eligible GTINs get a 14-digit left-padded grouping key. Other codes keep their raw value and never group.
- **CA-3** Catalogue nutrient values are stored as published text with unit, keyed by the source's own code; nothing is converted between sources.
- **CA-4** Imports run as a command in the app image, by hand on the VPS, once per archive. Archives stay in a server folder outside git. Every source row is counted as imported, already present or rejected with a reason.
- **CA-5** Search: PostgreSQL full-text search (`simple` configuration) on name and brand with a GIN index, plus exact GTIN lookup. Results group by GTIN and the account's links and show every member record with its own values. Trigram search is added only if portion 2 measurements show a need.
- **CA-6** Each confirmed link is stored as a pair `(account, record_a, record_b)`. An account's groups are the connected records over its links plus shared GTINs, computed with a cycle-safe recursive query. Removing a link deletes only that pair; remaining paths or GTIN matches keep records connected. With no other connecting paths, links 1–2 and 3–4 merge after adding 2–3 and separate again after removing it. Logged meals never change.
- **CA-7** Formats: Ciqual 2025 XML, BLS 4.0 ZIP (inner format inspected in portion 2), USDA full CSV and Open Food Facts JSONL.

**Chat tools and skill**

- **CH-1** Initial tool set: `list_accounts`, `read_diary`, `search_diary`, `save_meal`, `update_meal`, `update_entry`, `delete_record` (destructive), `save_product`, `save_recipe`, `list_saved`, `save_target`, `select_day_target`, `save_weight`, `search_catalogue`, `read_catalogue_record` and `set_food_link`. Reads are marked read-only. Portion 5 finalizes the schemas and verifies coverage of the approved behaviors.
- **CH-2** Each write returns the stored rows with IDs and versions; that response is the confirmation.
- **CH-3** Errors are JSON `{code, message, current?}`, passed through unchanged: `invalid_credential` 401, `account_not_available` 403, `not_found` 404, `version_conflict` 409, `request_id_reused` 409, `invalid_input` 400.
- **CH-4** Skill rules: name the account in every action and ask when unclear; use a new `request_id` per intended write and reuse it on retry; give each entry one source and a note; never save targets or link foods without explicit agreement; report success only from a returned record; on conflict, show both versions and ask.

**Implementation details recorded in the portion diffs (2026-10-17):**

- Deleting a usual product or recipe sets `product_id`/`recipe_id` on logged entries and ingredients to null; the entry's values, source and note stay as logged (DA-7), so no reference points at a deleted record.
- `update_entry` merges `nutrients` key by key (`null` clears one); other fields are replaced. The server never rescales values, so a portion change carries recalculated nutrients (skill rule).
- Adding, changing or deleting an entry advances its meal's `version`; entry writes return the meal's new version, so a meal update or delete based on an older read conflicts. Every write locks the meal row before its entries, the same order as a whole-meal delete's cascade, so concurrent writes cannot deadlock.
- A day's target-kind selection carries `target_kind_version` (returned by `read_diary`); changing an existing selection sends it as `version`, and a first selection omits it. Mismatches return `version_conflict` with the stored selection.
- The service-account file must be readable by the container's uid 1000 (`root:1000`, `0440`); `.env` stays root-only.
- Accounts store the Google display name from the verified token (refreshed at each website sign-in, never logged) so `list_accounts` can name A and B; it is not a secret.
- `read_diary` returns `{account_id, from, to, days[]}`, one item per day with `target_kind`, `target_kind_selected`, the effective `target`, `meals` with entries and `weights`. Adding entries to a meal returns the meal and `created_entry_ids`. `delete_record` uses `record_type` ∈ meals, entries, products, recipes, targets, weights. A second target of the same kind and start date is refused with `invalid_input`.
- Catalogue groups list their records (each with source, release, bases, the source's raw record type `data_type` where it publishes one, and published values, every value carrying its release's nutrient definition `name`) and the account's `links` among them. Search orders matches by full-text rank, then record ID.
- Import: `node apps/api/dist/import.js <ciqual|bls|usda|off> <path> --release <label>` inside the app container with `IMPORT_DIR` mounted at `/imports`; Ciqual takes the folder of its XML files. every USDA row is imported with its raw `data_type` (user decision, 2026-10-18), so laboratory sample and acquisition records stay distinguishable from foods, and market acquisitions keep the brand and UPC from `market_acquisition.csv`; Open Food Facts nutrients are keyed as published (see [research](research.md#verification-register)).

### Design

**Profiles and connections**

- **ID-1 — historical access-model migration:** migration `0006` deleted the then-existing personal test data, dropped `connections` and `grants`, and created `users` (Firebase UID, display name, the since-removed `password_allowed`, consent version and time) and the OAuth tables. Migration `0007` also cleared the then-existing personal test data and established exactly one profile per user: `accounts` keeps `owner_user_id` and `display_name`, drops `is_own` and the per-user name index, and becomes unique per user. It added `connection_profiles` (OAuth grant ↔ profile) and `connection_invites` (hashed one-time join codes), and keyed stored write results by grant. Profile-owned rows cascade on deletion. Catalogue tables, releases and archives were untouched. These describe completed historical transitions, not a future rollout step. The migrator skips already-applied migrations; never replay these resets against an existing diary. Any database that has not applied them needs a separately reviewed preservation plan before an upgrade.
- **ID-2** Naming: the API and tools say profile: routes `/api/v1/profiles/{profileId}/…`, fields `profile_id` and `name`, and error `profile_not_available` (403) for unknown and unreachable profiles alike. Internal table and column names keep `account`.
- **ID-3** Access: an OAuth caller may use only profiles linked to its grant; a website (Firebase) caller only its own profile, read-only. `ManageGuard` creates one user and profile at first verified Google or password sign-in, using the Firebase UID as identity. Both methods are public: the email/password form stays visible on signed-out diary, connect and join pages, with sign-up and Firebase password reset. No account allowlist, provisioning or moderation is needed. Migration `0008` drops the unused `password_allowed` column without resetting any data. Other providers are refused. The admin command can still create/reset a dedicated Firebase reviewer account; its profile is created on first sign-in like any other user's. At most 10 profiles per connection.
- **ID-4** Joining: `add_profile` creates a single-use code (`fdj_`, hashed, 30 minutes, at most 5 live per connection) and returns `https://<host>/join?code=…`. The join page shows who connected that ChatGPT connection, needs the joining person's own sign-in and consent, and links their profile. Each person can remove their profile from a connection; a connection with no profiles left is revoked.
- **ID-5** Deletion: the whole user with their profile, records, links, invites and the connections they authorized, then the Firebase user (Admin SDK). Permanent; the website asks for typed confirmation.

**OAuth (resource and authorization server in the API)**

- **OA-1** Endpoints on the same host: protected resource metadata at `/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource` and `/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource/mcp` (resource `https://<host>/mcp`, authorization server `https://<host>`, scope `diary`); authorization-server metadata at `/.well-known/oauth-authorization-server` (issuer `https://<host>`, `code`, `authorization_code` and `refresh_token`, `S256`, `client_secret_basic` and `client_secret_post`, `authorization_response_iss_parameter_supported: true`, revocation endpoint); `GET /oauth/authorize`, `GET /api/manage/oauth/request`, `POST /api/manage/oauth/decision`, `POST /oauth/token`, `POST /oauth/revoke`. No DCR or CIMD.
- **OA-2** One provided client, `chatgpt`. Its secret is generated on the server, stored as a SHA-256 hash in `.env` and entered once in the submission portal by the user. Redirect URIs match exactly against an `.env` allowlist, starting with `https://chatgpt.com/connector_platform_oauth_redirect`; T7 records the URI the portal actually uses.
- **OA-3** `/oauth/authorize` first validates the client and exact redirect URI; failures there get an error page, never a redirect. It then requires `response_type=code` and PKCE `S256`, and accepts `resource` only when it equals our MCP URL; other errors redirect with `error`, `state` and `iss`. Requested scopes are reduced to `diary`. A valid request is stored for 10 minutes; its ID goes into an HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax cookie scoped to `/api/manage/oauth`, and the browser is redirected to `/connect`, so only the browser that started connecting can approve it. `/connect` runs inside ChatGPT's sign-in browser, so it uses Firebase redirect sign-in (AC-6 option 3), never a popup; the page shows who is signed in and offers another account. Website responses send `frame-ancestors 'none'` against clickjacking of the consent screen. The decision, sent with a Firebase ID token, returns a single-use code (60 s, hashed) with `state` and `iss`, or `access_denied` with `iss`. Approval requires consent to the current health-data notice and records its version. The code exchange creates the grant and links the approving person's profile.
- **OA-4** Opaque tokens: `fda_` access (1 hour) and `fdr_` refresh (rotated on every use, expiring after 180 days unused), stored as SHA-256 and bound to grant, user and resource. A rotated refresh token presented again within 60 seconds (a retry after a lost response) gets a fresh pair; later it revokes the grant. The website lists only grants with a usable refresh token. One grant is one ChatGPT connection. A person's website lists the connections linked to their profile, with creation and last use, and removes their profile from one.
- **OA-5** `/mcp` and `/api/v1` accept valid `fda_` tokens for our resource, reaching the grant's linked profiles. `/api/v1` also accepts Firebase ID tokens on `GET` routes only, so the website reads through the same routes as chat; Firebase writes are refused. `/api/manage` accepts only Firebase ID tokens (AC-5). The SPA fallback (AR-1) also excludes `/oauth`, `/.well-known` and `/mcp`. A 401 from `/mcp` carries `WWW-Authenticate: Bearer resource_metadata="…"`; an auth failure inside a tool call returns `_meta["mcp/www_authenticate"]` with `error` and `error_description`.

**MCP, skill and package**

- **MC-1** The MCP endpoint is served by the API at `/mcp` (stateless, SDK v2). Tool definitions live in `apps/api/src/mcp` and call `/api/v1` on loopback with the caller's access token, so validation, idempotency, conflicts and errors are reused unchanged. Stored write results are keyed by grant (DA-9).
- **MC-2** `list_profiles` returns the connection's profiles (ID and name only); tools take optional `profile_id`; `add_profile` (`request_id`; `POST /api/v1/profiles/invites`) returns a join link. ### Live stage

A live verification batch requires separate approval. Human sign-ins, consent, reviewer credentials, recordings and publication remain user actions; a failure stops dependent steps.

1. Wait until the Open Food Facts import and its queued validation finish; do not interrupt them.
2. Verify the approved data-preservation and recovery policy. **User:** enable Firebase Email/Password sign-in, and confirm the intended Google Auth audience.
3. Deploy only the reviewed source and explicitly approved migrations and configuration. Never replay an old initial-data reset against an existing diary. Verify health, discovery documents, authorization validation and the challenge route.
4. **User, iPhone Safari:** sign in to the website and check every area, its states and the display totals with synthetic data.
5. **User:** create the OpenAI Platform organization and complete individual verification (the directory shows this name); choose a plugin name of at most 30 characters that is not a generic dictionary term; provide a support email; approve the legal pages.
6. Upload the ZIP and resolve metadata and skill-scan findings; verify the domain and connect the server; the **user** enters the client secret in the portal. Run T7.
7. Provision the reviewer account with synthetic data; the **user** enters its credentials in the portal and records the demo video. If T7 shows the draft is unusable in ChatGPT before approval, ask before buying a Business seat for recording. The **user** submits and accepts the attestations.
8. Fix review findings (T9). After approval the **user** publishes, choosing countries; then run T8 on the Plus iPhone.
9. After T8 passes, delete the private Site and plugin and the closed probe, with a separate OK (OP-9).

**Submission readiness:** use a dedicated reviewer account with synthetic data, verify fresh sign-in and OAuth consent, and rehearse the positive and negative package cases. Keep reviewer credentials and join codes out of source and recordings. Provide an actual reviewer-accessible demo URL and verify playback before submission. Recheck the existing portal draft, public pages, scan results and reviewer access. Submission and publication each require explicit authorization.

**Risks and limits**

- **Approval is outside our control.** Self-service onboarding for other personal accounts exists only after OpenAI approves and we publish; no documented fallback serves personal plans. The review may object to the DuckDNS host, the name, health-data handling or annotations; T9 records the outcome.
- **Development tools versus packaged skill.** Actual authenticated reads/writes through the new custom MCP development connection are confirmed as of 2026-10-20. The complete skill-plus-tools installation, reviewer-account host flow and published-plugin iPhone acceptance remain distinct unverified checks; the working development connection does not prove them.
- **iPhone connect flow.** Google sign-in inside ChatGPT's iOS sign-in browser is unproven until T8. If it fails there, connecting on chatgpt.com in Safari is the fallback; whether that connection then works in the iPhone app is not stated and is part of T8.
- **Adults only.** The user approved age 18+ on 2026-10-20. Sign-in and consent, policies, listing and skill state that scope; the skill does not manage children's diaries. Birth dates are optional after the approved 2026-10-21 repair; no identity documents are collected.
- **Security.** Our own OAuth server is security-sensitive. Portion 7 gets an independent review before deployment.
- **Backups.** None for this release; server loss can permanently lose personal data, as disclosed in the policy.
